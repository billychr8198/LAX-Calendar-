Your first briefing

Welcome, Chief Executive

Here is the whole idea, in plain words. It takes about two minutes to read and you only need to read it once.

You have just been made the boss of Los Angeles World Airports. LAX is a great airport, but it is not number one in the world. Your job is to change that — and the only way to do it is by looking after your own days first. Every day you keep your schedule, this airport gets a little bit better. Do that for four months and LAX ends up at the top.

Open the schedule

Go to the Schedule tab. You will see a calendar for September, October, November and December 2026 — 122 days in total. Tap any day to open it.

Write your list

Type what you want to get done and press enter. Each task appears on the departure board as a flight. Tap a task to edit it, tap the box on the left to tick it off, tap the × to remove it. It is your list, so put whatever you like on it — work, study, exercise, chores, anything.

Give each task a time slot

Every task can have a time block, written as 00h.00m - 00h.00m. Tap the time next to a task to set it, or use the two clock boxes beside the Add button. There are +30m, +1h and +90m shortcuts so you rarely have to type an end time at all.

You can also type the time straight into the task box — 08.00-09.30 Deep work — and it will be split for you. Tasks sort themselves into time order, a strip at the top of the board shows your day laid out end to end, and if two blocks overlap they are marked in gold so you can spot the squeeze. Times are completely optional: a task with no block is still a perfectly good task.

Tick things off during the day

Each task you finish changes from Boarding to Departed. If you would rather not retype the same list every morning, use Load my template, Copy yesterday, or fill a whole month at once.

Log the day

At the end of the day, press Log this day. That is what scores your points and opens a route. You can reopen a day later if you want to change something.

Watch a new route open

Every logged day opens the next route on the LAX board, with the real airline that flies it. They start small — a ski flight to Montana — and build up to Tokyo, Sydney, Dubai and London.

Come back tomorrow

That is the whole game. Log all 122 days and LAX becomes the number one airport in the world. You will know when it happens.

How points work

You do not need a perfect day to score. You just need to get past half.

122 days × 16 points = 1,952 points for a perfect season. Points decide your world ranking, which starts at #9 and climbs. #1 is reserved for someone who logs every single day.

What if I miss a day, or have a bad one?
Nothing bad happens. You can log a day with nothing ticked off — it scores zero points, but the route still opens and your season keeps moving. Missing days does not erase anything you have already earned. A short list you actually finish beats a long list you never touch.

Beyond the terminal

Discover Los Angeles

Around 88 million people pass through LAX every year, and a lot of them only ever see the inside of it. Here is what is waiting outside the doors.

What makes this city different

Los Angeles is not really one city. It is dozens of neighbourhoods that grew until they touched, spread across a basin between the mountains and the Pacific. That is why nobody agrees on what LA "is" — Santa Monica and Boyle Heights and Pasadena feel like different countries, and all three are twenty minutes from somewhere completely unlike them.

A few things everyone gets right: you can surf and see snow on the same day. More than 200 languages are spoken here. The food is the best argument for the place — tacos, Korean barbecue, Thai Town, Persian Westwood, Oaxacan Koreatown. And the light in the late afternoon really is like that.

Seven guides

Where to actually go

Start with the first if you have never been, then work down. Nothing loads until you press play.

Straight off the plane

South of the airport: Manhattan Beach and Hermosa Beach are ten minutes away — the closest real beach towns to the terminal.
North: Venice and Santa Monica, twenty minutes up Lincoln Boulevard. Boardwalk, pier, and the end of Route 66.
East: Downtown LA, Koreatown and the museums along Wilshire. The Metro C and K lines connect to the airport people mover.
If you only have a couple of hours: stay in the terminal and look at the Theme Building from the window. It has been standing there since 1961 and it still looks like the future.
